Air

Reading the air

Utah's air forecast gives each day two labels: how the air may affect your health, and what you are asked to do about it. This page explains both, and where the numbers come from.

The AQI in six colors

The Air Quality Index (AQI) is EPA's color-coded scale for outdoor air. It has six categories, each with a range of index values, and a higher number means more pollution and more concern. AirNow

For each pollutant, an AQI of 100 generally lines up with its short-term national health standard, and values up to 100 are generally seen as satisfactory. AirNow Above 100, the air is unhealthy first for sensitive groups, then for everyone as the number climbs. AirNow

The Utah Division of Air Quality (DAQ) names PM2.5 as the pollutant that builds up in winter and ozone as the summer one. Utah DAQ PM2.5 means fine particles, generally 2.5 micrometers across or smaller, about a thirtieth the width of an average human hair. EPA

Since May 6, 2024, Moderate starts at 9.1 µg/m³ instead of 12.1, and EPA expected many places to see more yellow days as a result. EPA The change reflects newer health science, not dirtier air. EPA

Ozone has its own ranges. Ozone is a gas that forms when pollutants react in sunlight. Utah DEQ Its AQI uses an 8-hour average in parts per million (ppm): Good runs up to 0.054 ppm, and Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups starts at 0.071 ppm. EPA

The AQI covers five pollutants: ozone, particle pollution, carbon monoxide, nitrogen dioxide and sulfur dioxide. EPA When several are measured, the AQI you see is the highest of their separate values. EPA AirNow's maps and readings use only ozone and particles for now. AirNow Pollen is not one of the five, so a green AQI says nothing about pollen. For that, see Pollen.

Who counts as sensitive depends on the pollutant. For particles, EPA names people with heart or lung disease, older adults, and children and teenagers. EPA For ozone, it names people with lung disease such as asthma, children and teenagers, older adults, and people who are active outdoors. EPA What to do at each level is on Breathe.

Health level and action level are two different things

Each day in DAQ's 3-day forecast carries two labels. Utah DAQ They answer different questions.

Health forecast

This is the AQI category for the day's highest expected pollution level. Utah DAQ It tells you how the air may affect people, so you can plan outdoor activity. Utah DAQ

Action forecast

This is Unrestricted, Voluntary or Mandatory, and it comes from Utah rule R307-302 for Box Elder, Cache, Davis, Salt Lake, Tooele, Utah and Weber counties. Utah DAQ DAQ also uses it to advise industry and drivers. Utah DAQ

VOCs (volatile organic compounds) and nitrogen oxides are the pollutants that react in sunlight to form ozone. Utah DEQ The ozone version of Mandatory does not mention stoves or fireplaces, so an ozone Mandatory day is about driving and VOCs. Utah DAQ

The two labels move separately. In the rule text, DAQ announces a mandatory no-burn period when PM2.5 is forecast to reach 25 µg/m³ or more. Cornell LII On the 24-hour scale above, 25 µg/m³ falls in the Moderate range, so a Mandatory day can come with a yellow Health forecast. EPA In Salt Lake County, a Voluntary day is also a no-burn day from November 1 to March 1. Salt Lake County More on that under Can I burn today?

Read both. The Health level tells you how hard to push outdoors. The Action level tells you what you are asked to do, from the woodstove to the car.

Can I burn today?

From November 1, check before you light anything. These are the rules as DAQ, Utah DEQ and Salt Lake County state them.

  • The season. DAQ usually calls no-burn days between November 1 and March 1, but it can call one at any time of year. Utah DEQ A Utah DEQ news release, first posted in 2018, says the wood-burning restrictions start November 1. Utah DEQ
  • Mandatory means no wood, pellets or coal. Wood fireplaces, wood stoves, pellet stoves and coal stoves may not be used, and that includes EPA-certified stoves. Utah DEQ Open burning is out too, including fire pits, fire rings and campfires. Utah DAQ
  • Gas and propane are allowed. Under the state rule, you may use natural gas appliances and propane stoves on no-burn days. Utah DEQ
  • One household exemption, with one wrinkle. DEQ names a single exemption: a stove registered with DAQ as the home's sole source of heat. Utah DEQ The registration has to be in place for it to count. Cornell LII The rule text also leaves out sources above 7,000 feet in Box Elder, Davis, Salt Lake, Tooele, Utah and Weber counties. Cornell LII If you think either applies to you, ask DAQ before you burn.
  • Salt Lake County goes further. From November 1 to March 1, the county bans solid-fuel fires in fireplaces and stoves, and outdoor fires such as bonfires, patio fire pits and charcoal grills, on Voluntary days as well as Mandatory ones. Salt Lake County Burning yard waste or trash is not allowed in the county at any time. Salt Lake County
  • Fines differ by source. DEQ's no-burn page lists fines of up to $150 per household. Utah DEQ The 2018 DEQ release says a first violation costs $150, later ones cost more, and penalties can reach $299 a day. Utah DEQ Salt Lake County lists penalties of up to $299 a day. Salt Lake County
  • Where the state rule applies. DEQ describes the area as all of Salt Lake and Davis counties, all of Cache Valley, Weber and Utah counties west of the Wasatch, Box Elder County from the Wasatch west to the Promontory range and south of Portage, and Tooele County between the north ends of the Oquirrh and Stansbury ranges and north of Route 199. Utah DEQ The rule text itself lists all of Utah County, not only the part west of the Wasatch. Cornell LII
  • Wasatch and Summit are outside it. R307-302 names seven counties, and Wasatch and Summit are not among them. Utah DAQ DAQ's Wasatch County forecast still shows an action level. Utah DAQ For any local burn rules there, ask your county.

Confirm the day's level at air.utah.gov. DAQ puts the action level on its website and gives it to local news outlets twice a day. Utah DEQ It also offers forecast emails, a phone forecast line at 801-536-0072 (toll-free 1-800-228-5434) and the UtahAir app for Android and iOS. Utah DAQ

Monitors, sensors and models

The numbers in apps and maps come from three kinds of sources. They are not equally solid.

  • Official monitors. DAQ runs a network of monitoring stations, listed by county on its station page. Utah DAQ Its live readings are 1-hour averages, updated once an hour and not quality assured. Utah DAQ AirNow says its data get only preliminary checks, not the full review used for official records. AirNow A daily PM2.5 AQI takes a full 24 hours of readings. EPA For particles, AirNow fills the gap with a NowCast: an average of the last 12 hours that leans toward the last 3 when the air is changing fast. EPA DAQ's current-conditions page shows a single hour in µg/m³. Utah DAQ Neither is the 24-hour average in the table above, so one yellow hour does not make a yellow day.
  • Low-cost sensors. Sensors such as PurpleAir cost less and are easier to run than regulatory monitors, and individuals own most of the ones on the AirNow Fire and Smoke Map. AirNow In a 2021 study by EPA scientists, raw PurpleAir readings ran about 40 percent high in most of the U.S. AMT EPA corrects sensor data before the Fire and Smoke Map shows them. EPA Fog, humidity and temperature can throw sensors off. EPA They also tend to read low for dust. AirNow EPA advises against putting AQI colors on minute-by-minute sensor readings, because the AQI rests on longer exposures. EPA
  • Model forecasts. Some air quality numbers come from a computer model, not a monitor. Open-Meteo, for example, serves the CAMS global forecast, which uses grid cells about 45 km (28 miles) across. Open-Meteo A strong inversion holds pollution in a shallow layer near the ground. Utah DEQ Models can struggle with that. In a 2021 study of January 2011 in the Salt Lake Valley, a research model roughly followed the rise and fall of PM2.5 during inversions but ran about a third too low on average, partly because it mixed the air more than the real atmosphere did. ES&T Clean days can be off too. On the morning of October 24, 2026, the Open-Meteo model put a point in Salt Lake City at about 12 µg/m³ in each hour through 9 a.m. Open-Meteo DAQ's Salt Lake County station measured under 4 µg/m³ in each of those hours. Utah DAQ Both links update, so later visits show newer numbers. Treat a model number as an estimate, not a reading. AQI forecasts usually come from state, Tribal and local forecasters. EPA In Utah, that forecast is DAQ's. Utah DAQ

One odd sensor

You may see one sensor in purple or maroon among green neighbors. A single sensor can sit beside a local source such as a fire pit, be labeled outdoors when it is indoors, or be shown at the wrong spot on the map. AirNow Nearby sensors rarely match exactly, but they should show similar trends. Stuck values or sudden jumps can signal a problem with the sensor. EPA Before you change plans, compare it with the sensors around it and the nearest official monitor. EPA If you notice smoke symptoms, take steps to cut your exposure, whatever a sensor reads. EPA

Which forecast covers you

DAQ forecasts by county. Utah DAQ AirNow reports by area, and an area's number for any hour is the highest AQI among its monitors. AirNow AirNow's "Salt Lake City" area takes in eight monitors, and one of them is Davis County's monitor in Bountiful. AirNow So AirNow's "Salt Lake City" number speaks for both counties.
