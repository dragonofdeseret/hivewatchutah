The year in Wasatch air

Each season brings its own kind of air. Most follow patterns you can plan around.

The year at a glance

The Utah Division of Air Quality (DAQ) says Utah's air is within federal health limits for most of the year. Utah DAQ The harder stretches follow the seasons, as the cards below show.

Tree pollen is a common allergy trigger in early spring. AAAAI

Why winter air gets stuck

Air usually gets colder as you go up. In an inversion, a layer of warmer air sits on top of cold air at the ground, and the two stop mixing. Utah DAQ

The Salt Lake Valley is a basin. The Wasatch Mountains, the Oquirrh Mountains and Traverse Mountain ring it, holding cold air in and blocking the stronger winds higher up that might otherwise sweep it out. Utah DAQ

PM2.5 means particles smaller than 2.5 microns across, about 3 percent of the width of a human hair. Utah DAQ

  1. Snow falls. Snow on the valley floor reflects heat, and long nights and a low winter sun keep the ground cold. Utah DAQ
  2. High pressure settles in. Sinking warm air caps the valley like a lid. The longer the high pressure stays, the stronger the inversion. Utah DAQ
  3. Particles build each day. Pollution from vehicles, wood burning, homes, businesses and industry stays near the ground, and PM2.5 keeps climbing the longer the inversion lasts. Utah DAQ
  4. A storm clears it. It often takes a strong storm or low-pressure system to mix the valley air out. Utah DAQ

A typical Utah winter has about five to six multi-day inversion episodes and, on average, 18 days with PM2.5 above the national health standard. Utah DAQ

Most of Utah's PM2.5 is not emitted as particles. It forms in the air when gases such as nitrogen oxides (NOx), volatile organic compounds (VOCs), sulfur dioxide and ammonia react. Utah DAQ

The main product is ammonium nitrate. It makes up about 40 percent of particle mass outside inversions and up to 70 percent during them. Utah DAQ

Estimates of where the pollution comes from use different methods and years, so the ones below do not add up to 100 percent.

  • Vehicles. In its 2009 to 2014 cleanup plans, DAQ put vehicles at almost half of the emissions that form PM2.5 during winter inversions. Salt Lake City puts vehicle exhaust at over half of Utah's air pollution on a typical winter day. Utah DAQ Salt Lake City
  • Homes and businesses. Smaller "area" sources, the everyday activity of homes, institutions and businesses, come second, DAQ's director told Congress in 2023. U.S. House
  • Large industry. In the same 2023 testimony, he put major smokestack industries at about 13 percent of the emissions inventory in Utah's areas that were then out of attainment for particles. U.S. House
  • Wood smoke. A University of Utah and DAQ study estimated wood burning at about 10 to 23 percent of winter PM2.5 at three stations in 2007 to 2014. It also found home wood burning fell by roughly 79 to 93 percent from 2007 to 2017. University of Utah

Winter is getting better

EPA approved moving the Salt Lake City and Provo areas into attainment for the 24-hour PM2.5 standard. The rule was published November 19, 2025, and took effect December 19, 2025. Federal Register

EPA had first listed those areas, along with Logan, as not meeting the standard on December 14, 2009. Utah DAQ

The Logan area in Cache Valley was redesignated earlier, in June 2021. EPA

The 24-hour standard is 35 micrograms per cubic meter (µg/m³). In 2024, EPA lowered the separate annual standard from 12.0 to 9.0 µg/m³. State of Utah

Utah told EPA that every regulatory monitor in the state had a 2022 to 2024 annual average below 9.0 µg/m³. The highest was the Near Road station beside I-15 in Murray, at 8.6 µg/m³. These figures were still pending final certification of the 2024 data. State of Utah Utah DAQ

Vehicles are getting cleaner. KUER reported in August 2026 that Wasatch Front Regional Council and DAQ data show vehicle emissions down 73 percent since 2011, even as total miles driven rose about 30 percent over 15 years. KUER

Attainment does not end inversion season. DAQ still calls no-burn days when fine particles build up during winter inversions. Utah DAQ

Summer ozone

Ground-level ozone is not emitted directly. It forms when NOx and VOCs react in sunlight and heat. Utah DAQ

Unlike winter haze, ozone has no color or smell. Utah DEQ

In Utah, ozone is usually highest between noon and 8 p.m., from May through September. Utah DAQ

The likeliest days are hot, sunny and still. Utah DEQ

Ozone can harm the lungs. Even low levels can bring on coughing, an irritated throat, chest pain or shortness of breath, and ozone may worsen asthma and other long-term lung conditions. Utah DEQ

EPA names people with lung disease such as asthma, children and teens, older adults and people who are active outdoors as the sensitive groups for ozone. EPA

Utah's state guidance adds people with heart conditions, people with allergies and people who work outdoors. Utah DHHS

A DAQ air monitoring manager suggests that people sensitive to ozone shift outdoor activity to the morning on high-ozone days, when levels are lower. Utah DEQ The Breathe page has more on planning around bad-air days.

The ingredients come from cars, trucks and other engines, from industry, and from small everyday sources such as gas lawn equipment, paints, solvents and household products. Federal Register Utah DEQ

A 2022 field study at a Salt Lake City monitoring site modeled four high-ozone days. On the two with little or no smoke, ozone was highly sensitive to cutting VOCs and barely changed when NOx was cut by up to half. University of Montana

The Northern Wasatch Front ozone area covers all of Salt Lake and Davis counties and parts of Weber and Tooele counties. It is designated nonattainment, meaning it has not met the 2015 federal ozone standard of 70 parts per billion (ppb). Utah DAQ

Wildfire smoke

Smoke can reach the Wasatch Front from a wildfire in another state, not just from fires nearby. Utah DAQ

Its fine particles are the biggest health concern. Utah DAQ

DAQ's director testified in 2023 that wildfire smoke brings the highest PM2.5 levels Utah's monitors record. U.S. House

Smoke can push ozone up too. Drawing on Utah's analysis, EPA counted 27 smoke-influenced days with ozone over the standard across the area's monitors in 2021, compared with 6 in 2022 and 8 in 2023. Federal Register

Smoke can rise and fall sharply over a single day. DAQ suggests waiting for cleaner air before you are active outdoors. Utah DAQ

The AirNow Fire and Smoke Map, run by EPA and the U.S. Forest Service, shows PM2.5 from federal monitors and thousands of sensors, along with fire locations and smoke plumes. EPA

Great Salt Lake dust

As of January 2026, about 800 square miles of Great Salt Lake's bed was exposed to the wind. University of Utah

On October 23, 2026, the South Arm stood at 4,189.7 feet, a provisional reading that may be revised. Utah DNR

The state's Great Salt Lake Strike Team uses 4,198 feet as the healthy level in its long-range projections. University of Utah

Most of the exposed lakebed has a fairly solid crust that makes it harder for dust to lift off. DAQ estimates that about 8 percent of it forms dust "hotspots" that may give off dust. Utah DAQ

A dust event usually passes quickly, often lifting particle levels for just a few hours. Utah DAQ

University of Utah researchers tested lakebed sediment in the lab. They found arsenic and lithium above EPA's screening levels for residential soil. The particles were also more reactive than sediment from other Great Basin dust sources, which the researchers see as a sign of greater potential harm to the lungs. University of Utah

Those are soil benchmarks applied to sediment, not measurements of the air, and the researchers said they could not yet tell whether these metals reach neighborhoods in blowing dust. University of Utah

DAQ says the area meets the federal standards for PM10 (particles 10 microns or smaller) and PM2.5. Utah DAQ

DAQ tests airborne particles for metals at only a limited number of stations. Over several decades, those samples have not shown rising arsenic or other toxic metals tied to dust events or to changes in lake level. Its PM10 samples from five Salt Lake-area sites in 2022 and 2023 had every metal below levels of concern. Utah DAQ

From 2023 to 2025, three of the seven stations closest to the lake recorded dust above the 24-hour PM10 standard of 150 µg/m³, four times in all. DAQ notes that not every dust event comes from the lake. Utah DAQ

A University of Utah model of four 2022 dust events found exposure highest for Pacific Islander and Hispanic residents and for people without a high school diploma, and lowest for white residents. In the model, a fuller lake lowered dust for everyone and narrowed that gap. University of Utah

Not everyone breathes the same air

A valley shares one air, but not in equal measure.

A preliminary EPA environmental justice review, presented in July 2023, found that Westpointe, Jordan Meadows, Poplar Grove, Glendale, Fairpark and Rose Park carry more pollution than the rest of the state because they sit near I-15, the airport and major rail lines. KSL

The same review reported a higher asthma burden, higher cancer risk and lower life expectancy there than on the city's east side. KSL

DAQ says west-side communities carry more of the valley's particle pollution because they sit close to sources such as the Inland Port, traffic, gravel operations and the drying lake. Utah DAQ

Its WestSide AirSense project planned 36 PM2.5 and 10 PM10 low-cost sensors there and was set to finish in May 2026. As of October 2026, its page still offers free email alerts when particle levels rise in west-side neighborhoods from Rose Park to Magna and West Valley. Utah DAQ

DAQ's Near Road station in Murray sits 14 to 19 meters (about 46 to 62 feet) from the center of the nearest I-15 lanes, placed there to track pollution from vehicles. Utah DAQ

It is the station with the highest 2022 to 2024 annual PM2.5 average on the state's list, noted above, though still under the standard. State of Utah

As of 2023, the Salt Lake area had five petroleum refineries. U.S. House

A year-long DAQ study in 2015 measured elevated formaldehyde and dichloromethane at its Bountiful Viewmont monitor in Davis County. The measured levels often carried an estimated cancer risk above a one-in-a-million threshold. Utah DAQ

In 2017, DAQ and the University of Utah sampled 34 sites in Bountiful, North Salt Lake, Woods Cross and West Bountiful, near refineries and other industry, to trace the sources. Utah DAQ

What Utah is doing

  • No-burn days. DAQ sets an Unrestricted, Voluntary or Mandatory level for wood, pellet and coal burning in Box Elder, Cache, Davis, Salt Lake, Tooele, Utah and Weber counties, or parts of them. On Mandatory days, wood, pellet and coal stoves and fireplaces are off limits, even EPA-certified ones, unless registered as a home's only heat source, and fines run up to $150. Natural gas appliances and propane stoves are allowed. Utah DAQ
  • Salt Lake County goes further. From November 1 to March 1, the county bans solid-fuel burning and outdoor fires, including patio fire pits and charcoal grills, on Voluntary days as well as Mandatory ones. Penalties run up to $299 a day. Salt Lake County
  • Summer ozone alerts. On ozone Voluntary days, DAQ asks people to combine trips and cut VOCs from products like paints and solvents, and on Mandatory days employers start required trip-reduction programs. Utah DAQ legend Utah DAQ forecast An older Choose Clean Air page, kept in DAQ's data archive, describes voluntary no-drive alerts from May 1 to September 30, mainly for Salt Lake, Davis, Utah and Weber counties. Utah DAQ
  • Tier 3 gasoline. This lower-sulfur fuel can cut tailpipe emissions by up to 80 percent in some cars. DAQ's director said in August 2026 that all suppliers now sell Tier 3 gas across the northern Wasatch Front. KUER
  • Idle-free Salt Lake City. The city bans unnecessary idling over two minutes on public property and on private property open to the public, such as drive-throughs and parking lots, with one warning before a fine. Salt Lake City
  • UCAIR grants. The Utah Clean Air Partnership (UCAIR) has funded emission-cutting projects since 2013. Its 2026 round gives priority to projects that help underserved or disproportionately affected communities. Grants go to organizations, not individuals. UCAIR
  • SmokeReadyUtah. DAQ and its partners plan indoor and outdoor sensors at about 40 public schools, and offer a limited number of free air cleaners to households in eligible zip codes. The project runs through March 2027. Utah DAQ
  • Dust signs and fees. H.B. 378, passed by the 2026 Legislature, covers commercial sites over a quarter acre that raise dust and need a DAQ dust control plan, such as gravel pits and earthmoving sites. They must post a public sign with a permit number and contacts, including DAQ's. The bill also lets DAQ charge rock, sand and gravel operations a yearly compliance fee starting July 1, 2027. Utah Legislature

For your own home, Prepare Utah's air guide covers furnace filters, a bedroom HEPA air cleaner and asking your clinician about an action plan for asthma or COPD. Prepare Utah

Common questions

When is air quality worst in Salt Lake City?

Utah’s Division of Air Quality says the air is within federal health limits for most of the year, and the harder stretches follow the seasons. In winter, inversions trap cold air in the valleys and fine particles (PM2.5) build up near the ground. In summer, ozone is usually highest from noon to 8 p.m., May through September, and wildfire smoke can drift in, sometimes from fires in other states. See The year at a glance.

Why does air get stuck in the Salt Lake Valley in winter?

In an inversion, a layer of warmer air sits on top of cold air at the ground, and the two stop mixing. The Wasatch Mountains, the Oquirrh Mountains and Traverse Mountain ring the Salt Lake Valley and hold that cold air in. Pollution stays near the ground, and PM2.5 keeps climbing the longer the inversion lasts, often until a strong storm or low-pressure system mixes the valley air out. See Why winter air gets stuck.

What causes air pollution on the Wasatch Front?

Most of Utah’s PM2.5 (fine particles) is not emitted as particles but forms in the air when gases such as nitrogen oxides, volatile organic compounds, sulfur dioxide and ammonia react. In winter, state and city estimates put vehicles at roughly half of the pollution, with homes and businesses second and large industry and wood smoke also adding to it. Summer ozone forms when gases from engines, industry and everyday products such as paints and solvents react in sunlight and heat. See Why winter air gets stuck.

Is winter air pollution in Utah getting better?

The Salt Lake City and Provo areas now officially meet the federal 24-hour standard for fine particles (PM2.5), under an EPA rule that took effect December 19, 2025. Vehicles are cleaner too, with state and regional data showing their emissions down 73 percent since 2011. Meeting the standard does not end inversion season, though, and the state still calls no-burn days when fine particles build up. See Winter is getting better.

Can I burn wood in my fireplace today in Salt Lake County?

Check the day’s action level at air.utah.gov before you light anything. On Mandatory days, the state rule bars wood fireplaces and wood, pellet and coal stoves, even EPA-certified ones, unless one is registered as a home’s only heat source. Natural gas appliances and propane stoves are allowed. In Salt Lake County, Voluntary days are no-burn days too, from November 1 to March 1. See Can I burn today?.

Does the AQI include pollen, and where can I find a Salt Lake City pollen count?

No. Pollen is not one of the five pollutants in the AQI, so a Good AQI day can still be a heavy pollen day. For a measured count, check the certified counting station at Intermountain Health’s Southridge Clinic in Riverton, whose count fits the Salt Lake Valley best. See Where to see a real count.

Does Great Salt Lake dust affect air quality?

Dust events can lift particle levels, often for just a few hours, and they peak from March to May. Utah’s Division of Air Quality says the area meets the federal particle standards, and its metal tests at a limited number of stations have not shown rising arsenic or other toxic metals tied to dust events. University of Utah researchers found arsenic and lithium in lakebed sediment above EPA’s screening levels for residential soil, but they could not yet tell whether those metals reach neighborhoods in blowing dust. See Great Salt Lake dust.

What should I do when wildfire smoke reaches Salt Lake City?

Limit your time outside, and because smoke can rise and fall sharply over a single day, wait for cleaner air before you are active outdoors. Indoors, close up one room while keeping a way out, run a portable air cleaner sized for it, and hold off on frying, candles and wood stoves. If you have to go out, the mask that works is a NIOSH-approved N95 or P100 respirator that seals over your nose and under your chin. See Make one clean-air room.
